What is this thing?

TG-16 owner's manual

A drum machine with no samples inside. Every sound is made from scratch in your browser by a small neural network, each time you press New pack. Nothing is sent to a server.

How a drum is made

Each pad starts as 512 random numbers. A generator model nudges that noise sixteen times toward the drum you asked for: a kick, say, in a deep house flavour, a little longer than usual. A second, smaller model turns the result into one second of audio.

All sixteen pads are made together, in about a second on a recent laptop.

Turning a knob or changing genre keeps each pad's noise and changes only the request, so the sound shifts but stays recognisably the same drum. New pack rolls fresh noise for everything.

What the controls ask for

  • Genre and machine are words the model learned: 27 genres and 22 drum machines. Each genre also sets length and tone on every pad. Those settings were chosen by hand, not learned.
  • Length, tone and pitch are properties measured on the training sounds: how long a hit lasts, how bright it is, and its strongest frequency. Each knob spans what was normal for that drum.
  • Tempo, swing, crunch, filter and level are ordinary audio controls. No model involved.

How it was built

  1. A large text-to-audio model, Stable Audio 3 (small-sfx), was asked for 50,004 one-second drum hits. The prompts were built from 172 words, each tested beforehand to check it changed the sound.
  2. A simple gate threw out clips that were clipped, silent or contained two hits. 48,741 remained.
  3. An autoencoder learned to squeeze each sound into 32 × 16 numbers and back again. Its decoder half is the one running here.
  4. A flow-matching model learned to produce those numbers from noise, given the drum type, the prompt words and the measured knobs.
  5. Both were exported to ONNX and run on this page with ONNX Runtime Web, on your graphics card where the browser allows it.

Known faults

The cowbells are not good. Kicks carry less sub-bass than a real 808. Every sound is one second of mono, and the machine only knows the sixteen drums on its pads.
